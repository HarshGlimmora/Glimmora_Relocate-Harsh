01 — What this project isA platform that moves a life across borders.

Glimmora Relocate is a software platform that helps a person move to a new country and build a complete life there — with an AI assistant that stays with them from the first thought of moving to the day they are fully settled.

Think of it as three products fused into one: a job platform that only shows you jobs you can actually take given your visa situation; a relocation service that helps you find a home, school, bank, doctor, and community in your new country; and a financial planner that tells you honestly whether the move makes sense on paper. Binding all three is an AI Copilot that understands your full situation — you, your family, your skills, your money, your constraints — and guides you through every decision and task.

It is not a set of disconnected tools. Every recommendation the Copilot makes is bookable, payable, and trackable inside the same platform. The same system that tells you “this apartment scores 92 in your neighbourhood” is the one that lets you book it, pay the deposit into escrow, and have the landlord’s SLA monitored until you get the keys.

One sentence: Glimmora Relocate takes a person from the decision to move abroad all the way to being fully settled — with one AI Copilot doing the thinking and one platform doing the execution.

02 — The problem we solveMoving abroad is broken into twenty pieces.

Today, relocating to another country means assembling twenty different services on your own — and hoping none of them drop the ball.

Someone who wants to move from India to Germany has to talk to a job board, a visa consultant, a tax advisor, a bank, a school search firm, a housing site, an insurance broker, a telco, a movers company, a language tutor, and a dozen government portals. None of these talk to each other. None of them understand the full picture of her life. Each one makes her re-explain who she is, what her passport is, how many children she has, what her salary target is. Each one solves a slice of the problem and leaves the joins to her.

The result: most people who want to move abroad never do. The ones who try often pick the wrong country, miss critical document deadlines, underestimate the true cost of living, or arrive and discover their family has no support. The process rewards people with money, time, and contacts — and penalises everyone else.

Glimmora Relocate exists because the fundamental problem is not a missing service. It is a missing intelligence. Nothing today reasons across jobs, visas, finance, family, and logistics as one coherent system. That is what we are building.

03 — The core ideaIntelligence plus execution, on one thread.

An AI Copilot that knows you, plans for you, and executes through a marketplace that actually delivers.

The product has two halves and they work together.

The intelligence half. An AI Copilot sits on top of a model of you — your Digital Twin — and a model of the world: a Country Knowledge Graph that encodes visa rules, tax rules, housing markets, school systems, cost of living, and the thousand little details that differ country to country. When you ask “can I afford to move to Berlin with my family?”, the Copilot does not search the web — it reasons over your twin and the graph and gives you a grounded, personalised answer with the numbers behind it.

The execution half. Every recommendation translates into a concrete task with a deadline. Every task that requires a third party — a landlord, a school, a bank, a mover, a tutor — is fulfilled through a Marketplace of verified partners. Payments for those services flow through Escrow: your money is held until the service is delivered. Partner performance is continuously scored, and bad actors are removed. What the Copilot plans, the platform actually delivers.

Recommend, book, pay, track, verify — all on the same thread, for the same user, by the same system.

This is why the SOW calls the product “AGI-native”. The AI is not a sidebar or a feature. It is the central organising principle: the Copilot is the user’s single point of contact with the entire machine.

05 — The seven pillarsCapabilities, not surfaces.

The SOW defines seven pillars. Each is a capability of the platform — a thing the system can do on the user’s behalf — not a page or a screen. Several pillars show up on the same screen; one pillar shows up on many screens.

Pillar 01 · SOW §4

AGI Copilot

The conversational spine of the product. Understands intent, reasons about options, plans multi-step execution, and explains its decisions. This is what the user perceives as “Glimmora”; everything else is what the Copilot uses.

Pillar 02 · SOW §5

Job Intelligence

Finds jobs the user can actually take, not just jobs they could theoretically do. Visa-aware matching, CV and cover-letter generation, interview coaching, readiness scoring, and a marketplace where employers meet visa-ready candidates.

Pillar 03 · SOW §6

Relocation Intelligence

Everything a life needs on arrival: housing, schools, banking, insurance, healthcare, utilities, transport, and community. Each sub-module is both an advisor (“this neighbourhood fits you”) and an executor (“here is the apartment, book it”).

Pillar 04 · SOW §7

Financial Intelligence

The truth-telling layer. Projects net salary, taxes, savings, and currency impact for any country under consideration. Turns vague hope (“Germany sounds nice”) into concrete numbers (“here is what you’ll take home after tax and rent”).

Pillar 05 · SOW §8, §15, §16, §17, §18

Life Setup Engine

The execution layer. Turns plans into tasks, tracks documents and their expiries, teaches language and culture, connects to local services, and runs parallel plans for the spouse and the children.

Pillar 06 · SOW §12, §13

Marketplace & Payments

The commercial surface. Verified partners list real inventory; users book; payments flow through escrow; subscriptions and commissions power the business.

Pillar 07 · SOW §14, §26

Trust & Compliance

The reason any of this is safe. Partners are verified before they can list. SLAs are scored continuously. Fraud is detected. Data is encrypted, access is controlled, GDPR is respected.

The three silent pillars. Three capabilities are invisible to the user but load-bearing for the platform: the Digital Twin (§9), the Country Knowledge Graph (§10), and the Data Flywheel (§20). They are not user-facing pillars — they are the substrate the seven pillars stand on.

10 — How the parts cooperateThe conductor and the orchestra.

The Copilot is the conductor. Every other module is an instrument it calls on — and the cooperation is choreographed, not improvised.

When a user asks the Copilot to do something non-trivial (“plan my move to Berlin”), the Copilot does not answer directly. It consults the user’s Digital Twin to understand context, queries the Country Knowledge Graph for rules and facts, uses the Financial Intelligence module for money math, and then hands a plan to the Workflow Orchestrator.

The Workflow Orchestrator breaks that plan into tasks with dependencies and deadlines. Tasks that need a third party are routed to the Marketplace. The Marketplace asks the Trust & Verification layer which partners are eligible to serve this user, and presents them in ranked order. When a user books, Payments & Escrow takes over — the money is held, the partner fulfils, the user confirms, funds release, the Trust score updates.

Underneath all of this, the Data Flywheel is watching every event and feeding signals back to the Knowledge Graph (“average time to get a Blue Card appointment in Berlin this quarter was 21 days, up from 14”) and to the matchers (“this landlord’s bookings convert worse for families than individuals”). The next user benefits without anyone writing new code.

Plan flows downward (Copilot → Workflow → Marketplace → Payment). Signals flow upward (Fulfilment → Trust → Flywheel → Copilot’s next answer).

11 — How the business makes moneyFour revenue streams, one product.

The same platform serves consumers and businesses, and earns from both. Diversity of revenue is on purpose: no single line dominates, and the B2B and B2C sides reinforce each other.

  1. Consumer subscription. A base plan at $100/year unlocks the full Copilot experience, document vault, and timeline execution. This is the entry point and the anchor.
  2. Premium tiers. Access to Verified Human Experts, priority support, additional family members, expedited services. Typical upsell once a user is deep in their journey.
  3. Marketplace commissions. A take-rate on fulfilled partner bookings (housing, schools, insurance, local services). Directly tied to execution — we earn when the user actually lands a service.
  4. Enterprise (B2B) mobility. Seats sold to corporates through the Corporate Mobility Portal. Higher price per user, contracted annually, with SLAs.

Fairness and trust are part of the business model, not just the product. Escrow protects the user; partner vetting protects the platform; SLA scoring protects both. If we push too hard on commissions, trust collapses — so the model is calibrated around repeatability, not one-shot extraction.

12 — The technical backbone, in plain EnglishWhat’s inside, explained without jargon.

You do not need to write code to be productive on this project, but you should know the shape of what is inside.

  • A Python backend (FastAPI). Python is where the AI and data libraries live; FastAPI is a modern, fast framework for building web APIs. Everything behind the scenes is a network of FastAPI services.
  • A React frontend. React is the world’s most common framework for building web apps. The Consumer App and all portals are built on it.
  • PostgreSQL as the main database. The industry-standard database for anything serious. Every user, job, partner, payment, and task lives here.
  • A knowledge graph for country data. Not a table of facts, but a network — “Germany” is connected to “Blue Card” is connected to “minimum income threshold” is connected to “software engineer”. This is how the Copilot reasons about the world.
  • Large Language Models (LLMs) behind the Copilot. We use commercial LLMs (Anthropic’s Claude is the default) to power the conversational layer, with guardrails and retrieval so the answers are grounded in our data — not the LLM’s guesses.
  • A workflow engine. Plans are long-running — some tasks take months. A durable workflow system runs them, retries what fails, and survives restarts without losing progress.
  • A payments processor with escrow. Likely Stripe or a comparable provider with Connect-style escrow, handling subscriptions, bookings, and payouts across currencies.
  • KYC/KYB for identity. Third-party services verify that people and businesses are who they claim to be, before any money or listing flows.
  • Event pipeline + analytics warehouse. Everything interesting that happens gets logged as an event; those events feed dashboards and the Data Flywheel’s learning jobs.
  • Cloud infrastructure with regional deployments. So a European user’s data can stay in Europe, satisfying GDPR and local regulations.

13 — Where the project is todayGreenfield, with a map.

As of 22 April 2026, nothing has been built yet. The repository contains only version control. What does exist is the plan.

The Statement of Work (SOW) is the contract on scope. The companion document glimmora-relocate-architecture.html is the reference architecture: information architecture, portal connections, detailed user flows, data model, and a sequenced roadmap. This handbook is the onboarding lens.

The first wave of real work, W0, is the foundations: authentication, the initial Digital Twin schema, a Country Knowledge Graph seeded with three launch corridors, and the Copilot runtime skeleton. From there the product expands outward through exploration (W1), jobs (W2), relocation plans (W3), marketplace and payments (W4), full life setup (W5), family and student modes (W6), enterprise (W7), and the flywheel at scale (W8).

Several open questions still need answers before execution — which countries we launch with, how we source world data, which LLM provider we commit to, how escrow licensing works in each jurisdiction. Those are detailed in section 11 of the architecture document.

Short version of status. We know what we are building. We have a reference architecture and a wave-based roadmap. We have not written code. The next real milestone is closing the open questions and standing up W0.

14 — GlossaryTerms you will hear, defined.

Anything in this list, you can use in conversation without further explanation.

AGI Copilot
The AI assistant at the centre of the product. “AGI-native” is aspirational wording in the SOW; in practice the Copilot is an orchestrated LLM with memory, tools, and guardrails. It is how users talk to the platform.
Digital Twin
A structured, evolving model of a user (or a family member, a job, or a relocation plan) that the Copilot reasons about. The twin is not a record — it is an interpreted, live representation of who the user is and where their journey stands.
Country Knowledge Graph (KG)
The platform’s model of the world. Encodes visa rules, tax rules, housing data, schools, cost of living, and the relationships between them. Enables reasoning, not just lookup.
Workflow Orchestrator
The component that turns a plan into a running sequence of tasks with dependencies, deadlines, and durable state. Survives restarts and can run for months.
Marketplace
The catalogue of verified partners (housing, schools, banks, insurers, services) whose inventory is bookable through the platform. Ranked by fit and trust.
Escrow
A payment pattern where a user’s money is held by a neutral party and only released to the partner after the service is confirmed delivered. Protects both sides.
SLA (Service-Level Agreement)
The commitment a partner makes about how fast and how well they will deliver. The platform measures each booking against its SLA and uses the results to update trust scores.
Trust Score
A continuously updated number representing how reliable a partner has been. Feeds the Marketplace ranker and can auto-pause listings if it falls too low.
KYC / KYB
Know Your Customer / Know Your Business. The legal process of verifying the identity of a user or a partner company before letting them transact on the platform.
Visa-aware matching
Filtering jobs so that a given user only sees opportunities where the employer’s sponsorship reality matches the user’s visa eligibility. The defining edge of the Job Intelligence module.
Job-Relocation Sync
The coupling that ensures a job offer immediately updates the relocation plan — destination city, salary, start date — so the whole plan follows the employment decision.
Data Flywheel
The feedback loop that takes signals from every completed journey and improves the Knowledge Graph, matchers, and predictions for the next user. The reason the product gets better over time on its own.
Timeline Intelligence
The module that predicts how long each milestone will take for this specific user in this specific country corridor — and alerts on likely delays before they happen.
FR (Functional Requirement)
A numbered requirement in the SOW (e.g. FR-1, FR-40). Used as anchors in planning documents so we can trace what we built back to what was asked.
Persona
A type of real-world user with motivations and constraints. Used for product thinking. There are nine in this system.
Role
A set of permissions inside the software. Used for engineering thinking. There are twenty-two in this system.
Portal
A dedicated application or surface for one set of personas (Consumer, Employer, Partner, Corporate, Admin, Public). There are six.
Corridor
A supported origin→destination country pair (e.g. India→Germany). The richness of the platform is measured in corridors supported to depth.
Pillar
One of the seven capability groups defined in the SOW (Copilot, Job, Relocation, Financial, Life Setup, Marketplace & Payments, Trust & Compliance).
