01

Land on the app

Entry can be direct, from the public site, from an employer invite, or from a corporate HR invite. The shell of the Consumer App loads with an anonymous Copilot preview.

Public → Consumer
02

Sign up

Email, Google, or Apple. Email verification is soft-gated — the user can continue to intake first. A minimal account record is created.

03

Mode selection

The user picks Individual, Family, or Student. This determines which Copilot intake script is loaded and which Twin schema is instantiated.

04

Copilot intake conversation

A short guided dialogue — not a form. The Copilot asks about origin country, target countries, profession and skills, family shape, passport and visa history, timeline, and budget. Each answer updates the Twin in real time.

Copilot Twin
05

Digital Twin v0 created

At the end of intake, a v0 twin exists and is stored. From here on, every other feature reads and writes to this twin.

06

Exploration (free)

Three tools are open before subscription: country comparison, financial simulation, and visa-aware job discovery. The user can roam for as long as they want — the Copilot answers questions throughout.

07

Commitment

When the user is ready to turn exploration into execution, they subscribe at the base tier. This unlocks the document vault, plan execution, bookings, and the Marketplace.

Billing
08

Apply to jobs

From Job Discovery, the user applies. The Copilot generates a tailored CV and cover letter from the twin. Applications cross the boundary into the Employer Portal.

ConsumerEmployer
09

Interview prep & live interview

If shortlisted, the user runs mock interviews with the Copilot and then takes live interviews scheduled by the employer. Feedback at each step updates the readiness model.

10

Offer & acceptance

On offer, the Copilot helps evaluate terms against the financial model and negotiate. Acceptance locks destination, salary, and start date into the Relocation Twin, which triggers the Plan flow (Part B).

01

Job-Relocation Sync

The offer data flows into the Relocation Twin — destination city, salary, start date, employer. The twin transitions from “exploring” to “committed”.

02

Plan expansion

The Workflow Orchestrator reads the Relocation Twin and expands the plan into a dependency-aware task tree. The country and visa type determine which sub-flows activate.

Workflow KG
03

Timeline & milestones

Tasks are scheduled against the start date. Timeline Intelligence predicts realistic durations per task given country, user profile, and historical flywheel data.

04

Documents

The Document module generates a per-country, per-visa checklist. For each document: upload → AI validation (OCR · schema · tamper check) → vault → auto form-fill where possible → submit to gov portal.

FR-50 FR-51
05

Housing booking

The Marketplace surfaces verified housing matches. The user books; the deposit is held in escrow. The booking request crosses into the Partner Portal.

ConsumerPartner
06

Schools, banking, insurance, health

Parallel booking sub-flows run for each life domain. Each follows the same pattern: match → book → escrow (where money is involved) → partner fulfils.

07

Utilities, SIM, movers

Shorter-horizon tasks closer to the move date. Often transactional rather than escrowed; still routed through the Marketplace for trust scoring.

08

All placed

When every critical task has a confirmed partner or a successful submission, the plan enters the “awaiting arrival” state. Part C picks up from here.

01

Cultural & language prep

Structured learning paths assigned by the Copilot. Short daily modules; cultural expectations (workplace behaviour, social norms) alongside language basics.

02

Family prep

Parallel plans for the spouse (job search or re-skilling) and each child (school start, language, friend-finding). Each family member sees the parts of the plan that concern them.

Family Twin
03

Logistics

Shipping, movers, flights, pet relocation. Lower-intelligence, higher-coordination tasks; the Workflow engine orchestrates them with hard deadlines.

04

Arrival

Landing day. The Copilot switches to “arrival mode” — shorter, more checklist-driven interactions tuned for someone jet-lagged and stressed.

05

Key handover & escrow release

When the user confirms the housing keys are received, the escrow on the deposit releases automatically to the landlord. This is the single biggest trust proof of the platform.

Partner
06

Registration & residency

Country-specific tasks: address registration, residency permit activation, local ID. Forms auto-fill from the vault wherever the country supports it.

07

Utilities, SIM, transport

Electricity, internet, mobile plan, transit pass. Often bookable as a bundle through Partner offerings; Copilot-chosen based on the user’s neighbourhood.

08

Community

The Copilot introduces the user to other Movers with similar profiles, language partners, interest communities. Family-mode adds school-parent and spouse-peer groups.

09

Settlement (ongoing)

The Copilot stays active indefinitely. It tracks document expiries, tax deadlines, insurance renewals, kids’ school milestones, and surfaces them as proactive alerts.

10

Data flywheel

Everything about the user’s journey — which partners delivered on time, which cost estimates were right, which bottlenecks hit — flows back into the system to improve the next user’s plan.

Cross-portal inputs & outputs

What the Consumer App receives
  • From Employer: published jobs, visa metadata, interview invites, offers.
  • From Partner: listings, availability, booking confirmations, fulfilment updates.
  • From Corporate: policy envelope, seat invite, approvals.
  • From Admin: updated Country Knowledge Graph, trust signals on partners.
  • From Public site: pre-filled intent (e.g. simulated country, salary range).
What the Consumer App sends out
  • To Employer: applications, interview availability, offer responses.
  • To Partner: booking requests, escrow deposits, fulfilment confirmations, reviews.
  • To Corporate: milestone progress, status, cost-to-date (aggregated).
  • To Admin: fraud signals, disputes, support tickets.
  • To Flywheel: every event in the journey as a signal.

01

Company signup

An Owner registers the company, provides basic details, and invites teammates. Until verification passes, no jobs can be posted.

02

Business verification (KYB)

Legal entity check, business registration documents, a primary contact. The Admin Console receives this in its Partner/Employer verification queue.

Admin
03

Team setup

The Owner assigns roles — Recruiters, Hiring Managers, Interviewers, Finance. Permissions are scoped from here.

04

Company profile & sponsorship policy

A public-facing profile (what candidates see) plus an internal policy stating which countries the company sponsors, which visa tiers, and any restrictions. This is the input to visa-aware matching.

05

Create a job

Standard job posting plus structured visa fields: eligible passports, sponsorship tier, relocation benefits. Optional ATS sync pushes the same record to the employer’s existing system.

06

Publish

Once published, the job enters Job Discovery in the Consumer App. Only candidates whose twins pass the visa filter will see it.

EmployerConsumer
07

Applications & AI ranking

Applications flow in. The Matcher ranks them on fit and visa-path confidence; the Recruiter sees them ordered with reasoning badges.

08

Shortlist, interview, decide

Standard interview loop — scheduled through the Portal, feedback captured as scorecards. Rejections surface back to the candidate’s Readiness module as signal.

09

Offer

Created inside the Portal, pushed to the candidate’s Consumer App. Negotiation happens in a structured thread rather than email.

10

Acceptance → relocation triggered

On acceptance, the employer’s job is done. On the candidate’s side, the Job-Relocation Sync fires and the full plan unfolds automatically.

01

Signup & category

A Partner Owner registers and picks a category: housing, school, bank, insurer, local service. Each category has different document requirements.

02

KYC + KYB

Individual identity (for the signer), business registration, licences relevant to the category (real estate licence, education ministry accreditation, banking licence excerpts, etc.), and bank details for payouts.

Admin review
03

Admin review

A Trust & Verification Officer in the Admin Console reviews the submission. Approved partners unlock listings; rejected ones get a structured reason.

04

Create listings

Housing units, school slots, insurance plans, service packages. Each has pricing, availability, photos where relevant, and structured metadata (bedrooms, neighbourhood, language of instruction, coverage limits).

05

Publish to Marketplace

Listings go live in the Consumer App’s Marketplace. They are ranked by fit + Trust score; a freshly approved partner starts with a neutral score and earns ranking through fulfilment.

06

Booking request

A user books. The Partner Portal shows the request with the user’s relevant context (moving from / to, family shape if disclosed) — not their full PII.

From Consumer
07

Accept or decline

The Fulfilment Agent accepts, declines with reason, or counter-offers. Accepting converts the booking into an escrow-held contract.

08

Escrow holds

The user’s funds are held by the platform’s payment partner. Neither the Partner nor the user can unilaterally move them.

09

Fulfil the service

Hand over keys, confirm the admissions slot, activate the account, deliver the service. The Partner marks fulfilment in the Portal and the user is prompted to confirm on their side.

10

Confirmation & escrow release

When the user confirms, escrow releases to the Partner (minus platform fee). If the user disputes, it escalates into the Admin Console’s dispute workflow.

Dispute fallback
11

Trust update

The completed booking, time-to-fulfil, user review, and any incident feed into the Partner’s Trust score. This affects future ranking. A score that falls below threshold auto-pauses listings and alerts the Admin.

Receives

11 — Complete journey walkthroughPriya’s move, told end to end.

Meet Priya. 34, senior backend engineer in Bangalore, married to Rohan, mother to eight-year-old Ananya. She wants to move the family to Germany. This is what her time with Glimmora Relocate looks like, from first click to a life in Berlin.

Stage 01 · Discover — Public Site

Weekend, 9 months before move

Priya searches “move to Germany from India” on a Saturday morning. She lands on a Glimmora guide that actually has useful numbers, not fluff. In the side panel is a salary simulator. She enters her role, seniority, and current salary. The tool shows her take-home after Germany’s taxes, rent in Berlin, and an honest monthly leftover. She reads two case studies and opens the country comparison tool. She is impressed enough to leave her email for updates.

Stage 02 · Explore — Public Site → Consumer App

Next weekend

She clicks an email link and lands inside the Consumer App, signed up with a single click. The Copilot greets her by name and references the simulations she ran last week. Over twenty minutes, it walks her through an intake conversation — her family, her skills, her timeline — and a Digital Twin v0 is built. She spends the rest of the afternoon comparing Germany vs Netherlands vs Portugal. The Copilot explains trade-offs with actual numbers tied to her Twin: “With your profile and salary target, Germany has 3.4x more open roles but higher tax; Portugal has better family tax breaks but your salary expectation is above the market.”

Stage 03 · Commit — Consumer App

Two weeks later

She is sold on Germany. She subscribes to the $100/year plan. This unlocks the document vault, plan execution, and access to the Marketplace. The Copilot offers to include Rohan and Ananya as linked family members; she sends Rohan an invite and adds Ananya’s record (minor, no separate login).

Stage 04 · Hunt Job — Consumer App ↔ Employer Portal

Weeks 3–10

In Job Discovery, she sees only roles where the employer’s sponsorship policy matches her profile — this is the visa-aware matching at work. She applies to nine roles over six weeks. The Copilot tailors each CV and cover letter from her Twin. Three companies shortlist her. She runs mock interviews inside the app, then takes live interviews scheduled through the Employer Portal by recruiters on the other side. Feedback from rejections tunes her readiness score.

Stage 05 · Get Offer — Employer Portal → Consumer App

Week 11

A Berlin fintech sends her an offer. Inside her Consumer App, the offer lands as a structured card — base, bonus, equity, start date, relocation benefit, visa path. The Copilot runs it through the financial model and shows her Germany-equivalent take-home plus an explicit delta vs her current Bangalore package. She negotiates through the structured thread; the employer adjusts. She accepts. A moment later, Job-Relocation Sync fires: Berlin, EU Blue Card path, 1 October start date now live in her Relocation Twin.

Stage 06 · Plan Move — Consumer App

Weeks 12–14

Her dashboard transforms. Instead of exploration tools, she now sees a timeline anchored on 1 October, populated with milestones: Blue Card appointment (week 18), housing booked (by week 22), school place for Ananya (by week 24), bank account (week 26), flight (week 38). The Workflow Orchestrator has resolved dependencies automatically — she can’t book the apartment before her employment contract is signed, so that task is blocked until then. The dynamic document checklist shows her fifteen documents she’ll need, with statuses.

Stage 07 · Execute — Consumer App ↔ Partner Portal

Weeks 15–34

She starts executing tasks. She uploads her degree certificate; the AI validator checks the document, catches that the Apostille translation is missing, and tells her how to fix it. She re-uploads; it passes into the vault.

In Housing, the Marketplace surfaces twelve matched apartments in Berlin ranked by her neighbourhood fit (family-friendly, near an international school). She shortlists three, books one. The deposit — €3,200 — flows into escrow. On the Partner Portal side, a Berlin property manager accepts the booking and confirms key handover for 28 September.

For Ananya, the School matching surfaces two English-language primary schools with open slots; Priya completes admissions via the Marketplace. For the bank, she begins a remote account opening through a German neobank partner. Insurance is bundled. Rohan, meanwhile, uses his linked account to start his parallel job search — the Family Intelligence module runs his plan inside Priya’s.

Stage 08 · Arrive — Consumer App ↔ Partner Portal

27 September — 4 October

They land in Berlin on 28 September. The Copilot has switched to arrival mode: shorter, checklist-driven. The property manager meets them at the apartment; Priya confirms the keys in the app. The escrow releases automatically — the landlord’s bank account lands the rent deposit within the hour. The app’s first-week checklist starts firing: register the address at the Bürgeramt, activate the SIM card (ordered through the Marketplace, delivered to the apartment), buy a month’s transit pass, pick up her Blue Card. Ananya starts school; a Copilot-suggested playdate with another child from the platform is scheduled for the weekend.

Stage 09 · Settle — Consumer App

Ongoing

Three months in, Priya’s app looks different again. The timeline’s active tasks are gone; in their place is a steady-state view: her Blue Card renews in 47 months (the Copilot will nag her at month 45), her tax filing is due in April, Rohan’s interview prep for a local role is active, Ananya’s parent-teacher conference is on the calendar. The Copilot is no longer a planner — it is an assistant that knows everything about her family’s life in Germany and surfaces what matters proactively.

Behind the scenes, Priya’s journey is now feeding the Data Flywheel. The next Indian engineer moving to Berlin with a family of three will get a slightly sharper plan — realistic timing for the Blue Card appointment in her district, a better neighbourhood rank for family-friendly units, a truer estimate of first-year tax. She did not only complete her journey; she made the platform measurably better.

Why this walkthrough matters. Every portal shows up in it. The Public Site acquires her. The Consumer App is her home. The Employer Portal is the other side of her job hunt. The Partner Portal is how every real-world service gets delivered. The Corporate Portal is absent in this story (Priya is self-funded) — but if her employer had an enterprise contract, it would sit underneath her journey as a policy envelope, visible to her HR team. The Admin Console watched over every trust-critical moment (partner verification, document validation, any disputed payments). That is what a single-platform view looks like from the user’s chair.
