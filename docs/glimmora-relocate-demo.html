01 · What this project is

One company, one operating system for moving a life.

Glimmora Relocate is a multi-sided marketplace + AI copilot for cross-border professional relocation. Every time a person moves country for a job, five different kinds of user touch the process — the person, their future employer, the vendors who make life work on the ground, the HR team (if it's a corporate move), and the platform operators who keep trust intact. Glimmora is the single system that connects all of them.

Each portal is a separate Next.js application with its own database and its own domain (in dev: port). They interconnect through typed, Bearer-authed internal APIs and a shared identity layer. The frontend you're about to see is production-complete; the backend handoff is what this document prepares you for.

02 · The problem today

Cross-border relocation is twelve fragmented SaaS tools pretending to be a workflow.

When someone moves country for work, they use: a job board, an immigration lawyer's portal, a furnished-apartment site, a bank's PDF form, a school's PDF form, a tax consultant's email thread, a moving company's spreadsheet, a forum for expat advice, and a WhatsApp group of friends who moved before. Employers use a separate ATS. Corporate HR teams run the whole thing on another separate mobility tool. Nothing talks.

Consumer pain

What the person feels

  • Six months of admin, scattered across 30+ logins
  • No single view of “am I on track?”
  • Re-typing the same facts into every form
  • Family planning happens in parallel, privately, in notes apps
Market pain

What the industry feels

  • Employers sponsor 8 nationalities but review 50
  • Partners fight for qualified leads — 80% of inbound is tire-kicking
  • HR mobility teams run on Excel + email threads
  • Every country change in law propagates in months, not hours
The insight
Every stakeholder already has a workflow tool. They don't need another one. They need every stakeholder's tool to know what the others are doing — in one real-time data graph, with an AI that answers the hard questions.
03 · The solution

A shared digital twin, a copilot that grounds in it, a marketplace that knows who qualifies.

Each consumer has a Twin — a structured model of their life, goals, family, finances, and constraints. Every feature in the platform reads from and writes to the Twin. The Copilot never hallucinates a generic answer; it grounds every response in Twin data + the Country Knowledge Graph. Every marketplace surface is visa-filtered before it renders.

04 · System architecture

Six portals, one graph, typed APIs between them.

Each portal is a separate Next.js 14 App Router application. Each owns its own Prisma schema and SQLite database (in dev) — this is deliberate: each audience has a fundamentally different data model, authn boundary, and deployment cadence. Portals talk to one another through internal REST APIs authenticated with shared bearer tokens, or via the Admin-side cross-DB read layer (xdb.ts) which lets ops staff see the whole platform in one view.

05 · Why six portals, not one

Each audience has a different job to be done.

A consumer opens the product after dinner on their phone to worry about their kid's school. A recruiter opens it at 9am on Monday to fill a role. A partner opens it when a booking notification fires. An HR lead opens it once a month for a policy review. An ops officer opens it at 9am to clear a queue. None of these surfaces can share a sidebar, a home page, or a mental model. So they don't.

The portal separation logic

Separate applications — separate deploys

Portal-per-app means: the Consumer app can ship five times a week without affecting the Admin console. The Employer portal can have its own rate-limiting, feature flags, and release cadence. Failure domains are isolated — a bug in Partner doesn't take down hiring traffic. Each portal's bundle stays lean because it only ships the components its audience needs.

Why not a single monolith with role-based routes?
Because the mental model, navigation, empty states, and information density differ too much. Forcing one app to serve “Priya worrying about Ananya's school” and “Reya approving a €35k expense” produces a worse experience for both. The cost of maintaining separate apps is small; the cost of a confused user is permanent.
06 · Why every portal has its own public marketing page

Acquisition is a different product from the product.

A signed-in dashboard is optimised for depth: many inputs, many widgets, low ceremony. A public landing page is optimised for the opposite: convert a stranger into a believer in 40 seconds. Same brand, same data eventually, completely different intent — so they live as different surfaces with different layouts, different metadata, different SEO concerns, and often different A/B testing.

Why all four public pages live inside the Consumer app

The public site shares the Consumer codebase because it shares the same font stack, the same country knowledge graph, the same marketing imagery, and the same SEO domain. The public page is /, and /for-employers is a sibling route — but the CTA on that page points to the separate Employer app's sign-up URL. So the user never re-authenticates, they never land in a generic “pick your role” funnel, and each audience gets a tailored onboarding.

07 · Data flow across portals

One person's move lights up four databases.

When a candidate accepts an offer, the row starts in the Employer database, propagates as a Relocation into the Consumer database, generates booking activity in the Partner database, and surfaces as a fee event in the Admin audit log. This diagram shows the canonical write flow for that single event.

Offer acceptance — write propagation across four databases
sequenceDiagram autonumber participant C as Consumer participant E as Employer DB participant CB as Consumer DB participant P as Partner DB participant A as Admin (read-only) Note over C,E: Candidate accepts offer C->>E: POST /api/offer/accept E->>E: Offer.status = ACCEPTED
Application.stage = HIRED E->>CB: POST /api/internal/relocation (Bearer) CB->>CB: Create Relocation
+ 8 RelocationMilestones Note over CB,P: Consumer books a housing service CB->>P: POST /api/internal/booking (Bearer) P->>P: Booking.status = REQUESTED
escrowState = HELD Note over P,A: Admin sees everything live A-->>E: SELECT hires A-->>CB: SELECT relocations A-->>P: SELECT bookings, payouts

Cross-DB read pattern — admin/lib/xdb.ts

Admin doesn't own user-facing writes on employer/consumer/partner rows. Instead it reads directly from their SQLite files in dev (in prod, replaces with read replicas or a warehouse) using better-sqlite3 opened in readonly mode. Verification decisions (approving KYB, toggling employer verified) use a separate write handle on the target DB, journaled with WAL so they don't contend with the owning portal's writes. All of this is encapsulated in 14 typed functions — this file is the single source of truth for how data flows into Ops.

14 · User journey

Priya · self-funded move from Bangalore to Berlin.

The main persona. Priya is a senior backend engineer with her husband and one child. She's moving on her own initiative — no employer relocation package, no HR team behind her. Every portal lights up in her story.

01discover

Public site · Compare & simulate

She lands on glimmora.ai from a Google search. Opens /compare, sorts corridors by tax + savings. Runs /salary for €85k gross IN→DE. Reads the Germany Guide. Signs up.

02plan

Consumer · Twin + Dashboard

Onboarding builds her Twin: 28, senior engineer, married, one child, target Germany, 6-month runway. Copilot proposes a 9-month plan. Every subsequent surface (Finance, Family, Documents) reads the Twin.

03apply

Consumer · Career → Employer portal

In /app/discover she sees only jobs whose employer's sponsorship policy accepts Indian passports. Applies to a role at Kontra GmbH. On the Employer side, her application lands visa-prequalified.

04interview

Employer · Interviews & Offers

Kontra runs the loop inside the Employer portal. Scorecards, offer sent, Priya accepts. Application.stage=HIRED fires.

05relocate

Consumer · Relocation created

Internal API propagates a Relocation row into Consumer DB with 8 milestones. Priya's Dashboard now shows Berlin as destination. Plan timeline becomes executable.

06execute

Consumer · Marketplace → Partner portal

She books a furnished apartment from Havn Housing (partner), an international school seat from Nordic Schools, a legal package from Pathway Legal. Each booking creates a row in Partner DB with escrowState=HELD.

07settle

Partner marks fulfilled · escrow releases

Landlord confirms move-in. Havn marks fulfilled. Escrow RELEASED in Partner DB. Payout queued. Priya's Dashboard shows that milestone complete.

08observed

Admin sees the whole story

Admin/Ops console shows: 1 new hire at Kontra, €7,600 success fee owed, Priya's relocation at 60% progress, 3 bookings settled, Havn trust score +1.

21 · Integration contracts

How portals talk to each other.

Two patterns: synchronous HTTP for writes that cross portal boundaries (e.g. Employer tells Consumer about a hire), and direct cross-DB reads for Admin-side aggregation. Both are typed with Zod.

Pattern A — internal HTTP with Bearer auth

Example: when an Employer accepts an offer, Employer's server action calls Consumer's internal API to create a Relocation.

Production swap-in

In production, pattern A stays the same (HTTP + Bearer), backed by real service endpoints. Pattern B swaps from better-sqlite3 on shared files to read replicas or a warehouse (BigQuery / ClickHouse). The xdb.ts function signatures stay stable — the implementation changes.
